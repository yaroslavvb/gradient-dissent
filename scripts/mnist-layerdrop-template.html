Quick definitions

Mask: four switches, first → last branch; 1 = kept, 0 = skipped. Checkpoint: saved model weights after an epoch (one pass through training data). Seed: a repeat with different training randomness. MAC: one multiply-and-add; a count of arithmetic, not elapsed time. CE: cross-entropy loss; lower is better. Two-branch resilience: how often an originally correct prediction survives the six ways to keep two optional branches.

What survives Fable’s argument?

Stochastic depth trains the model to cope with missing computation. That is a useful operational idea. A residual addition alone does not guarantee interchangeable layers: h + (A − I)h = Ah can rotate or completely change the representation.

Measure the deleted direction

A small residual norm helps only relative to the downstream decision boundary. We test the actual post-ReLU change, its downstream gradient, and norm-matched random directions.

Separate logits from loss

Cross-entropy can create interactions even when logits are additive. We measure both spectra and actually evaluate fractional layer gains, rather than treating exact interpolation of binary corners as proof of linear behavior.

Distinguish training and deletion

During training, a surviving SD branch is divided by its survival probability. This audit deletes branches at inference while keeping surviving gains at one. Those subnetworks have different scales.

These networks taper from 2500 to 500 hidden units. A bypass crops coordinates; it is not an equal-width identity or a clean ODE time step. Removing layers also changes the amount of computation, unlike merely using a coarser discretization of the same time horizon.
