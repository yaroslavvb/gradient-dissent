# Interpretation checklist for the frozen A100 study

Prepared 9 September 2026 while the tuning grid was running, before final test results. This is an interpretation audit, not a statement that the effects below were observed. The executed authority is [protocol.json](protocol.json), with implementation details in [README.md](README.md), [language-notes.md](language-notes.md), and [vision-notes.md](vision-notes.md). No new experiments were run for this checklist.

## What a positive result would add

- [ ] Say that the **specified layer-dropout recipe reduced pruning damage in the tested architecture/task**, provided the raw full/pruned quality supports that statement. Do not present transfer beyond GPT as discovery of stochastic depth outside language modeling. The original stochastic-depth work trained convolutional residual networks with randomly omitted layers; LayerDrop subsequently demonstrated transformer subnetworks that could be pruned without fine-tuning. [Huang et al., 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09382); [Fan et al., 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.11556).
- [ ] Identify the useful distinction: the GPT arm enlarges the earlier causal-model test; ViT tests bidirectional image attention; ConvNeXt tests convolutional residual blocks. The two vision arms share CIFAR-100 and its split, so they are not evidence from two independent image domains. No weights or representations transfer between these models; “transfer” means replication of an effect across settings.
- [ ] Treat constant ILD in ConvNeXt as a closely related established baseline. The official ConvNeXt implementation already includes a linearly depth-increasing DropPath rate. This study replaces that mechanism with its explicit mask path; it does not add a fundamentally foreign operation to a dense-only architecture. The new question is the comparative full/pruned behavior of these fixed schedules and masks. [Official ConvNeXt implementation](https://github.com/facebookresearch/ConvNeXt/blob/main/models/convnext.py).

## Read the primary endpoint together with absolute quality

- [ ] Display `D = CE(pruned) − CE(full)` and the paired `D(dropout) − D(dense)`, alongside both raw CEs. Negative paired differences indicate less damage. A weak model can have low damage simply because there is little useful computation to remove; lower damage alone does not establish a useful smaller predictor.
- [ ] Report image top-1 accuracy and absolute percentage-point changes as well. Better classification CE does not automatically mean a meaningful accuracy improvement. Distinguish an improvement in full-model CE from a pruning-robustness result.
- [ ] Compare effect direction across families, not raw CE magnitudes. Language losses are nats per GPT-2 token, image losses nats per example, and their tasks/output spaces differ. Avoid a pooled “average transfer gain.”
- [ ] Use three paired final seeds as the inferential units. A 95% Student-t interval uses df=2, critical value 4.30265273. Show individual differences and mark six primary family/recipe comparisons as unadjusted if no correction is applied. Nine masks or thousands of scored examples are not additional independently trained models. The intervals condition on the selected LR and fixed split; they exclude tuning-seed, architecture and dataset uncertainty.
- [ ] Do not infer that an eight-block or thinned model beats directly training that smaller architecture: this A100 protocol has no separately trained smaller-model control. The earlier local study's smaller controls do not fill that gap at the new scale.

## Decreasing schedules: a precise persistence claim

- [ ] A useful pruned final DTS model would demonstrate robustness at the endpoint of a schedule that reaches zero dropout. The final training step is exactly dense; there is **no separate long dense continuation**. Say “survives annealing to zero,” not “survives prolonged dense retraining.” The reviewed paper asks this endpoint question in Section 8.1.1. [Don't Drop Dropout, Section 8.1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05275v1).
- [ ] Constant `.4` and decreasing `.8→0` schedules have the same .2 mean omission, but differ in initial amplitude, variance, per-layer exposure over time and interaction with the LR schedule. Their comparison does not isolate an effect caused only by the final dense step, only by noise removal, or only by a curriculum.
- [ ] Growing subnetworks also have earlier precedent in RaPTr. A positive DTS result is compatible with that broader family of approaches; it does not establish a unique mechanism or novelty claim for a decreasing schedule. [Panigrahi et al., Efficient Stagewise Pretraining via Progressive Subnetworks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.05913).
- [ ] Do not generalize a ranking of constant versus decreasing ILD into a universal schedule optimum. A disagreement with the target paper on these tasks limits transfer of its ranking; it does not falsify the paper's measured comparisons on different architectures/data/budgets.

## ConvNeXt requires especially careful language

- [ ] Call its primary intervention **stagewise thinning**: retain 2/2/6/2 of 3/3/9/3 residual blocks, while every stem/downsampling transition still executes. GPT and ViT retain a prefix of 8/12 blocks. The common fraction refers to residual-block counts, not equivalent computational interventions or a global early exit.
- [ ] Report learned LayerScale values together with full-model quality. ConvNeXt initializes each residual multiplier at `1e−6`; an underused residual path may be easy to delete. However, LayerScale magnitude alone does not measure residual contribution: `gamma * f(h)` also depends on learned weights and activations. A large change in gamma is not proof of functional importance; a small gamma is not proof of irrelevance. No direct residual-activation attribution is measured here. [Official ConvNeXt block and initialization](https://github.com/facebookresearch/ConvNeXt/blob/main/models/convnext.py).
- [ ] State that CIFAR images are bilinearly resized from 32 to 64 pixels after augmentation. No new image information is created. The resulting stage geometry is 16→8→4→2, different from canonical 224-pixel ConvNeXt. A successful result is about this adaptation; a null result does not settle ImageNet-scale behavior.
- [ ] Do not attribute a CNN result to the paper's shared attention/FFN-mask interaction. GPT/ViT have two residual updates sharing a mask; ConvNeXt has one composite residual branch. These implement related stochastic-depth interventions, not identical local algebra.

## Training budget and learning sufficiency

- [ ] Report the actual budgets: GPT has 104,857,600 token presentations for 124,439,808 parameters, approximately **0.84 tokens per parameter**. This is much shorter than the target paper's 20-TPP setting. Completing the frozen 3,200-step schedule does not demonstrate convergence or full-scale GPT-2 pretraining.
- [ ] Describe vision sampling accurately: 2,621,440 ViT presentations and 5,120,000 ConvNeXt presentations correspond to about 58.25 and 113.78 passes over 45,000 training examples, **with replacement**, not shuffled epochs. A repeated small dataset is different from a modern single-epoch language-model stream.
- [ ] Use absolute quality and the learning trace to assess whether each family learned. A null robustness result in a weakly learned model is ambiguous. Conversely, do not call every unexpected outcome “undertraining” without evidence. The present data do not isolate model-size, duration, optimizer and dataset effects.
- [ ] Each recipe has an equal three-rate full-duration validation search on one tuning seed. This is better than unequal searches, but it is not exhaustive hyperparameter optimization. Disclose boundary winners and avoid post-test grid expansion or seed substitution. Different selected LRs mean final comparisons concern **equally tuned recipes**, not an intervention at identical LR. No claim should depend on omitting a failed or adverse final seed.

## Work, masks and inference claims

- [ ] “Same budget” means matched steps, batches and example presentations within each family. Expected residual-block omission is .2; training computes every branch before applying masks. No measured 20% FLOP, time, energy or dollar saving follows. Blocks can also have unequal costs, and mandatory components remain.
- [ ] These are nine predefined interventions per model, including only three sampled first-block-preserving masks. Do not describe the larger study as exhaustive subset evaluation or a guarantee over arbitrary pruning. First-block deletion was never sampled during ILD training and is a distinct stress test.
- [ ] Keep full and pruned evaluation at inference scale one, using the original normalization/readout and original block order. The frozen code has no auxiliary exit loss, adapter, head refitting or test-selected deployment mask. The original paper distinguishes prefix exits from intermediate skipping and finds that preferred mask distributions can differ between them. This study has no alternating-dropout training arm at A100 scale. [Don't Drop Dropout, Sections 8.1.1–8.1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05275v1).
- [ ] Improved pruned CE is not evidence of latency gains, correct speculative verification, acceptance rate, or lossless decoding. Those experiments are absent. Report A100 timing and memory as provenance/planning measurements unless a separate controlled systems comparison exists.
- [ ] Keep the previous mathematical caveat intact: inverse-survival scaling does not make the preferred shared-mask, two-sublayer transformer block globally expectation-equivalent to dense evaluation. A positive empirical result does not repair that derivation. [Existing mathematical audit](../../research/paper-audit.md#critical-issue-1-the-expectation-argument-fails-for-the-preferred-block-implementation).

## Wording once all final runs finish

If supported by the numbers: “At these fixed budgets, the equally tuned ILD recipes reduced damage from the predefined block deletions in [families]. The effect [did/did not] preserve useful absolute model quality. These results extend a known stochastic-depth mechanism to the tested settings; they do not establish universal transfer, an optimal schedule, or measured efficiency.”

Name mixed or inconclusive families explicitly. Keep reservations, metered usage snapshots and settled charges separate in the spending summary; scientific replication counts should likewise separate tuning, development pilots and final seeds.
