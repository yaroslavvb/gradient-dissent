# Auditing Fable’s explanation of layer dropout

September 9, 2026. This note evaluates the supplied Fable comments, not new experimental results. Its strongest proposal is to measure **interactions between omitted branches and downstream sensitivity**. Its four conceptual models are useful conditional explanations, but several identities and “if and only if” claims are false. The MNIST diagnostics below can distinguish some of these explanations without retraining.

## What survives scrutiny, and what needs correction

**1. Residual form permits bypassing; it does not prevent re-encoding.** For any linear transformation A, choosing f(h) = (A − I)h makes h + f(h) = Ah. This includes rotations, permutations and projections. More generally, f = g − Id expresses any representable g. A common vector space does not guarantee stable feature meanings or that downstream activations remain in their training distribution. Elhage and colleagues’ communication-channel description motivates a read/write analysis; it does not impose a mathematical restriction on re-encoding. [Transformer Circuits, 2021](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html).

This distinction matters especially here. The Ciresan adaptation uses **ReLU(P h + f(h))**, where P crops to the next, smaller width. Cropping and ReLU are retained when a branch is omitted. These are not invertible identity maps, and the stem and classifier remain mandatory. See [the actual model](../experiments/ciresan_stochastic_depth/model_data.py). The four-branch all-off model is therefore a particular stem–crop–head classifier, not an empty network. Elhage’s bigram interpretation concerns a zero-layer transformer with no cross-token computation; it is not a universal baseline for every architecture.

**2. Inverse-survival scaling gives a local expectation identity, not an equivalence theorem for successful dropout.** Conditional on fixed h and f(h), with m ~ Bernoulli(ρ), E[h + mf(h)/ρ] = h + f(h). A nonlinear downstream map need not preserve this equality. Affineness along the segment is sufficient for equality of expected downstream outputs; it is neither necessary for that equality nor an “iff” criterion for prediction quality. Cross-entropy of mean logits also differs from mean cross-entropy.

For this MNIST model, the identity holds before the post-addition ReLU. For the paper’s transformer, attention and FFN share a gate and execute sequentially, so it does not generally hold for the complete block either: scalar identity branches with input 1 and ρ = 1/2 yield training outputs 1 or 9, mean 5, versus dense output 4. This is an algebraic counterexample to a mean-preservation explanation, not evidence the method fails. [Paper, Eq. 6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05275v1); [existing audit](paper-audit.md).

Every function of **one binary presence variable**, with other variables fixed, already has a degree-one representation. That statement cannot establish approximate linearity on a continuous gate segment. Multiple-branch interactions and continuous interpolation curvature are distinct, measurable quantities.

**3. Read/write chains are an exact linear-model calculation, not a general circuit identification.** In an ordered product of rank-one residual matrices, the cross term for k < ℓ is indeed ε² uℓ(vℓᵀuₖ)vₖᵀ. Overlaps permit serial interactions, although input/readout projections and cancellation can eliminate their output contribution. This toy term alone does not identify an induction head: actual induction circuits also require token routing and the relevant attention computations. Hydra reports compensatory responses after ablation; it does not establish that compensation consists solely of independent redundant writers. [Elhage et al.](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html); [McGrath et al., 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15771).

Angular distance is **not** relative increment norm. If f(h) = 9h, the angle between h and h + f(h) is zero although ||f(h)||/||h|| = 9. Small angles primarily measure transverse change and can miss radial changes. Gromov et al.’s similarity-based pruning is evidence for a useful statistic in its tested models, not this claimed equality; its headline pruning results also include QLoRA healing. [Gromov et al., 2024/2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.17887).

**4. The ODE analogy requires additional assumptions.** Removing steps without enlarging retained steps shortens the horizon for an autonomous Euler scheme; it is not simply coarsening the same fixed-horizon integration. Depth-dependent vector fields add a sampling problem. Inverse survival preserves a conditional increment mean, not an entire stochastic trajectory. A global first-order error bound needs regularity and a bound on local truncation error; a Lipschitz constant alone does not supply the stated universal degradation curve. [Chang et al., 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.10348).

For the scalar test equation y′ = λy, forward Euler is absolutely stable precisely when **|1 + τλ| ≤ 1**. The condition τ|λ| ≤ 2 is the negative-real-axis special case. Purely imaginary nonzero λ gives an unstable Euler iteration for every positive τ. Neither the stability boundary nor O(τ) convergence proves that trained-network accuracy must decline as k/(L − k) or exhibit a cliff. [Driscoll and Braun, §11.3](https://fncbook.com/absstab-diffusion/).

Bartlett, Evans and Long’s Theorem 1 assumes a differentiable invertible map, Lipschitz Jacobian on a bounded domain, Lipschitz inverse and positive orientation. Its bound is **O(log m/m) on the Lipschitz seminorm of hᵢ − Id**, not O(1/m) on displacement. Constant translations illustrate why the two differ. This representation theorem does not characterize all functions learnable with droppable layers, nor prove that many-to-one operations must occupy mandatory early/late layers or that depth-increasing dropout is optimal. [Theorem 1 and its discussion](https://arxiv.org/html/1804.05012v2#S3).

**5. Path ensembles and contraction are conditional models, not interchangeable proofs.** Products of linear residual operators, and products of local Jacobians at a fixed activation state, admit path expansions. A general nonlinear residual network is not literally the sum of all independently evaluated subnetworks: f(a + b) need not equal f(a) + f(b). Veit et al. measured gradient contributions concentrated in relatively short paths; those are not nonnegative portions of prediction loss. Their 10–34-layer range corresponds to 5–17 two-layer blocks. It does not establish a universal positive “loss-mass” mixture or an iff robustness criterion. [Veit et al., 2016](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2016/file/37bc2f75bf1bcfe8450a1a41c200364c-Paper.pdf).

If the **same** κ-contractive map has a fixed point, L iterations obey ||eL|| ≤ κᴸ||e₀||. After k deletions the analogous bound is κᴸ⁻ᵏ||e₀||: the ratio of these upper bounds is κ⁻ᵏ, not necessarily the ratio of actual errors. Distinct contractions with different fixed points need not commute. A negative Lyapunov exponent is not a necessary-and-sufficient criterion for finite-depth classification robustness. DEQs study an equilibrium formulation; iterative-estimation and layer-deletion observations do not prove contraction in arbitrary untied networks. [Bai et al., 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.01377); [Greff et al., 2016/2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.07771). The cited Lad–Gurnee–Tegmark paper is titled *The Remarkable Robustness of LLMs: Stages of Inference?*, not *Transformer Layers as Painters*. [Actual paper, 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19384).

**6. Sensitivity is measurable; dropout does not enforce the proposed sufficient conditions.** For actual post-block deletion displacement δ and downstream composite loss G, Taylor expansion gives G(z + δ) − G(z) ≈ ∇G(z)ᵀδ + ½δᵀH_G(z)δ, with an appropriate smoothness/remainder qualification. Downstream amplification is already inside these derivatives; multiplying them again by a downstream Lipschitz product double-counts that dependence. For post-addition ReLU, δ = ReLU(P h) − ReLU(P h + f(h)), not generally −f(h). ReLU boundary crossings make finite differences more reliable than an unrestricted quadratic approximation.

Dropout optimizes an expected masked loss; it does not guarantee small Jacobians, nonexpansiveness or distributed redundancy. Inverse survival can enlarge the retained branch Jacobian. CompleteP concerns depth/width parameterization, feature learning and hyperparameter transfer, not a blanket contraction certificate. [Dey et al., 2025/2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01618).

## An exact interaction analysis for four MNIST branches

Walsh and Möbius expansions are related **different bases**, with different probability formulas. Let S be the set of present branches and ℓ(S) a fixed-checkpoint loss. The unique presence-bit expansion is

\[
\ell(S)=\sum_{T\subseteq S}a_T,\qquad
a_T=\sum_{U\subseteq T}(-1)^{|T|-|U|}\ell(U).
\]

For independent common survival q, Eℓ(S) = Σ_T a_T q^|T|. For exactly r uniformly retained branches, the coefficient instead is (r)_d/(L)_d for d = |T|, where (r)_d is a falling factorial and the value is zero for d > r. Under unequal independent survival qᵢ it is ∏_{i∈T}qᵢ. These follow directly from the probability T ⊆ S. Coefficients are signed; cancellation matters.

By contrast, the uniform Walsh basis uses zᵢ = 2mᵢ − 1 and coefficients multiplying ∏zᵢ. Independent Bernoulli(q) averaging weights degree d by (2q − 1)^d. The familiar noise-operator factor η^d concerns correlated bit perturbations, not the same one-way deletion experiment. Squared Walsh coefficients describe spectral energy; signed Möbius coefficients should not be presented as that energy. [O’Donnell, *Analysis of Boolean Functions*, Chapters 1–2](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~odonnell/papers/Analysis-of-Boolean-Functions-by-Ryan-ODonnell.pdf).

Four branches give only **16 masks**, so compute these exactly. Low degree alone does not imply robustness: a single arbitrarily large first-order effect can dominate. Cross-entropy itself can create higher-order interactions even when logits are additive; inspect logits or fixed true-versus-alternative class margins as well. Uniform-mask statistics must be distinguished from the unequal, time-varying training mask distribution. A training-time average of products is generally not the product of average survival probabilities.

## Predictions to register before inspecting diagnostic outcomes

1. **Robustness versus full quality.** At existing validation-selected checkpoints, compare residual dense, constant SD, annealed SD and residual unit-dropout over all 16 fixed inference masks, with no survivor rescaling. Report absolute CE/accuracy and damage relative to each model’s own full output. Predict that SD lowers mean fixed-depth ablation damage; separately test whether that improvement sacrifices full-model quality. The plain network is an architecture/performance control, not a compatible branch-ablation control.
2. **Interaction hypothesis.** Predict that SD reduces harmful joint deletion effects. Show each pair’s excess loss over the sum of singleton deletions, all interaction orders, and individual masks. Also calculate interactions in per-example logits or fixed class margins to separate network interactions from nonlinear loss. Do not interpret a smaller normalized high-order fraction alone as better robustness: its denominator and signed cancellations can change.
3. **Geometry versus downstream sensitivity.** Compare angular change, relative norm of the actual post-ReLU deletion displacement, and directional loss change against observed singleton damage. Predict that angle alone misses damaging radial changes and that sensitivity adds information. Four layers within a seed are not independent experimental replicates; report seedwise results and avoid a significance claim based on pooled layer/example counts.
4. **Interpolation hypothesis.** Vary one continuous branch multiplier across 0, 1 and its training survivor scale, with other branches fixed and unit dropout disabled. Measure logit/CE curvature and the conditional endpoint-versus-dense gap. Predict smaller gaps for SD as an exploratory mechanism, not a required condition. This needs an explicit diagnostic forward path because the standard model’s eval method deliberately keeps every branch.
5. **Compensation and mask shape.** Track the actual ablation perturbation through downstream stages and compare prefix/tail/interior omissions at matched retained counts. Predict that some perturbations attenuate or become irrelevant to the head; failure of contraction does not refute accuracy robustness. Do not reorder the tapered layers: their dimensions are incompatible. The all-off classifier is an essential measured endpoint.

Use a fixed validation panel for exploratory mechanism work, keep the already selected training/checkpoint protocol intact, and disclose any later confirmation on the official test set as post hoc diagnostics. These tests do not establish a universal taxonomy of droppable functions. The claims that parity/grokking tasks have no approximation ladder, that a binary-erasure-channel rate bounds neural-network capacity by ρL, and that every model must degrade gracefully then collapse require additional models and assumptions. Expected active depth is a computation count, not such a capacity theorem. A decreasing schedule ending at zero removes masking at the end; it does not prove that capacity or redundancy has been recovered.
