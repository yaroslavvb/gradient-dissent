# Practical Ciresan stochastic-depth training: what the experiments establish

This is a read-only synthesis of the saved source, pilots, tuning, final runs and diagnostics. No additional experiments were run. File paths below are relative to `experiments/ciresan_stochastic_depth/` unless explicitly linked otherwise.

**The smallest demonstrated successful package** is the existing tapered MLP with four shape-compatible residual bypasses, pixels divided by 255, a linear classifier head, ordinary SGD with momentum 0.9 at LR 0.01, and the specified stochastic-depth masks. It needs no normalization layers, special initialization, auxiliary losses, learned projections, warmup or sophisticated optimizer. This is a sufficient tested configuration, not proof that every individual change is necessary or that it is the mathematical minimum.

The main practical difficulty was making the residualized raw-input model train reliably. Once the normalized/linear-head recipe and stable LR were fixed, both SD schedules completed all three confirmation seeds: roughly 57 seconds of A100 training for 100 epochs. The more elaborate CUDA Graph implementation was for speed and correct conditional optimizer behavior; an earlier eager pilot already learned successfully.

## The exact architectural change

The supplied source is a plain six-affine-layer MLP, widths 784→2500→2000→1500→1000→500→10, with 11,972,510 parameters. `SimpleFullyConnected2` creates default-initialized `nn.Linear` layers. With the training script's default `nonlin=1` and `last_layer_linear=False`, **the final classifier also gets a ReLU**. `TinyMNIST` supplies raw floating-point pixels; its 28-pixel path does not divide them by 255. The script hardcodes seed 1 despite exposing a seed argument. Its optional `weight_decay` is a post-SGD multiplication by 1−coefficient, not the same-number SGD L2 penalty. These are properties of the supplied historical script, not a faithful recreation of Ciresan's original augmentation-heavy record model. Sources: [reference/train_ciresan_new.py](../experiments/ciresan_stochastic_depth/reference/train_ciresan_new.py), model creation, seed and optimizer blocks; [reference/util.py](../experiments/ciresan_stochastic_depth/reference/util.py), `SimpleFullyConnected2`, `TinyMNIST`; [history/source-audit.json](../experiments/ciresan_stochastic_depth/history/source-audit.json).

The main adaptation keeps the stem and head mandatory. Each middle layer maps

`h → ReLU(P(h) + M·(W h+b)/(1−p))`, where `P(h)=h[..., :out_features]`.

The crop adds no parameters, but it discards coordinates and changes the full network even when p=0. Therefore SD must be compared with **residual dense**, not only with the original plain MLP. The latter comparison confounds adding bypasses with dropping branches. The linear head and pixel scaling are common to all five main arms.

The mask is one independent Bernoulli per eligible layer **shared across a minibatch**. Constant SD uses p=[.1,.2,.3,.4]. Annealed SD starts at [.2,.4,.6,.8], multiplies by `1−e/99` over zero-based epochs 0…99, and ends at zero. Both average one skipped hidden affine layer per minibatch over training: 25% of the four eligible branches, 20% of the five hidden affine layers, or 16.7% of all six affine layers. The nominal affine-MAC saving is 14.626%, not 20%, because the most expensive branch is dropped least. Evaluation keeps all branches at scale 1. Ordinary unit dropout, by contrast, independently drops hidden units at p=.2 after all five ReLUs, including activations on the bypass route.

The graph helper captures all 16 masks but uses shared weights, gradients, momentum and survival-scale storage. Capture warmup restores weights/optimizer/RNG state before real training. Only active parameters enter SGD; optional historical shrinkage still applies to all parameters, even dropped ones. Main shrinkage is zero. These semantics were checked by six passing A100 qualification tests, including changing masks/scales, skipped-momentum freezing and fresh unit-dropout draws. Graph reciprocal multiplication can differ by rounding from the old eager scalar division; the old and resumed implementations are not claimed bit-identical. Sources: [train_optimized.py](../experiments/ciresan_stochastic_depth/train_optimized.py), `probabilities` and epoch loop; `optimization/stochastic_graph.py`; `results/graph-qualification-v1.json.kernel_validation`.

## What caused the early failures—and what remains unproved

The first A100 pilot using raw pixels, output ReLU and crop residuals had validation CE 2.3025855 and accuracy 10.22% at both epochs 1 and 5; its 2048-example validation probe had 100% zero logits. The otherwise source-style **plain** pilot reached 97.88% validation accuracy at epoch 5. Thus failure was already present without SD; attributing that collapse to stochastic masks would be incorrect. Sources: [pilot-source-residual.json](../experiments/ciresan_stochastic_depth/results/pilot-source-residual.json) and [pilot-source-plain.json](../experiments/ciresan_stochastic_depth/results/pilot-source-plain.json), `spec`, `history`.

The saved 20-update CPU diagnostic used the same initialization and first training batches as that pilot. With raw inputs and a ReLU head, adding residuals raises initial classifier-preactivation RMS 0.3666→26.6079, initial CE 2.3064→28.1710, and total gradient norm 7.7925→873.7183. Residual/raw/ReLU reaches 100% zero logits and zero CE gradient on the fixed 64-example probe by update 20. The head's existing momentum norm is still 501.54, so **zero current CE gradient is not proof of permanently frozen parameters**.

The head-only residual/raw/linear case avoids zero logits, but its first two updated probe losses are 309.88 and 919.47 before falling to 1.8675 at update 20; removing the head gate does not fix the large initial scale by itself. Normalized residual/ReLU and residual/linear instead end that short probe at CE 1.5826 and 0.7036. However raw cases use LR .001 and normalized cases LR .03, so this is not a one-factor input-scaling ablation. A 20-update training probe is also not a final generalization experiment. Exact locations: [collapse-diagnostic.json](../experiments/ciresan_stochastic_depth/results/collapse-diagnostic.json), `cases[id].fixed_first_batch_probes`, `protocol`, `algebraic_example`, `interpretation_limits`.

The algebraic failure is precise: for ReLU logits, `dCE/dz_j=(softmax(ReLU(z))_j−1[j=y])·1[z_j>0]`; if every preactivation is negative, the CE gradient vanishes. But a long-run absorbing-state claim is refuted by the fidelity observations: raw/ReLU residual seeds 102/103 eventually escape the near-chance plateau, finishing at 36.83/39.05% test accuracy. Seed 101 stays 9.80%. Raw/ReLU constant SD stays 9.80% for all three seeds through 100 epochs. Plain/raw/ReLU finishes 98.28/86.87/88.62%. Those mixed outcomes establish seed sensitivity for this package, not universal inability to train raw inputs or ReLU classifiers. Files: `results/graph-fidelity-source-{plain,residual,sd}-s101..103.json`, `final_test`, `history`.

The separate GPU seed 104 head diagnostic is stronger causal evidence for a particular head failure: only `output_relu` changes, yet digit 6 correct predictions rise 0→945 of 958. Still, the ReLU class 6 preactivation is positive on 1.98% of inputs and its head-row gradient is 1.49e−6, rather than identically zero. This failure concerns classification with the full network; it is not evidence about which digit tolerates subsequent layer deletion.

## How difficult was successful training?

An eager normalized/linear annealed-SD pilot reached 97.58% validation accuracy in five epochs, with 7.649s of training; the residual-dense pilot also reached 97.58%. This already established feasibility before the speed work. The first Torch 2.8 tuning campaign was interrupted when the user prioritized baseline optimization; archived transport/error outcomes are not additional evidence of mathematical divergence. The resumed frozen 15-run grid used Torch 2.14 and matched CUDA Graph execution for every arm. Three real divergences occurred at LR .1: constant SD epoch 12, annealed SD epoch 1, unit dropout epoch 21. All four SD tuning runs at LR .01/.03 completed 100 epochs. All six final SD runs at the selected .01 completed, and all reached 100% full-training accuracy by epoch 100. Files: `pilot-normalized-{residual,sd}.json`; `graph-tune-*.json`; `selection.json`; `graph-eval-*.json`.

The stabilized main recipe uses batch 64, SGD momentum .9, a constant learning rate, no shrinkage, and the fixed 50,000/10,000 split. Each epoch contains 781 updates and 49,984 examples. It therefore needs neither the separate baseline's BF16/B256/dropout/LR-decay bundle nor 100 epochs merely to begin learning. SD saves about 14.3–14.4% at a fixed 100-epoch horizon, consistent with the actual branch skipping. It does **not** reach this quality threshold faster: residual dense is quicker. The primary checkpoint minimizes validation CE, not test error. Primary paired accuracy effects are −.570 percentage points for constant SD (95% t CI [−.958,−.182]) and −.377 for annealed SD ([−.931,+.177]); final-epoch effects are +.043 ([−.033,+.119]) and +.110 ([−.004,+.224]). Final CE is worse for both SD arms. Intervals use the three paired seeds, df 2, on one reused test set; they are conditional, uncorrected for multiple comparisons, and not population intervals. Exact sources: `graph-eval-{residual,sd_constant,sd_annealed}-s101..103.json`; `results/analysis.json.paired_comparisons`.

The later 18 instrumented replays exactly match all original recorded scientific curves; the 15 main replays also match initialization/order/mask hashes. Their probes confirm near-complete fitting and concentrated late gradients, not evidence that SD eliminates overfitting. See [telemetry/training-findings.md](../experiments/ciresan_stochastic_depth/results/telemetry/training-findings.md). Distinguish those conclusions from depth robustness: good full-depth accuracy, survival after dropping a layer, and useful image-specific routing are separate endpoints, analyzed by the exhaustive-mask study.

## Practical recommendations, with their evidential limits

For a simple reproduction, use the frozen main recipe and residual control; keep the stem/head, add crop bypasses, use linear logits and normalized pixels, and start from the already-tested LR .01. This reproduces a successful package. Do not present it as a proof that normalization, that exact LR, the crop choice, or annealing are indispensable.

Inspect per-class predictions and logits before blaming SD for stalled learning. That check directly found the seed 104 head problem. Check dense validation quality alongside pruning quality and wall time. Keep momentum behavior consistent when skipping branches. CUDA Graphs are optional for the scientific experiment and useful for this small-kernel A100 workload; they do not repair a failing optimization recipe.

A controlled test of raw/linear residual SD, normalized/ReLU SD, smaller residual gains, alternative projections, clipping or normalization layers would be new evidence. None should be described as an established minimum fix. The separate optimized plain baseline's 3/3 raw-linear and 3/3 normalized-linear target successes do not transfer automatically to SD, and its repeatedly monitored official-test target does not provide a pristine generalization benchmark.
